# Layout choices

Select a form by asking what the reader needs to follow, compare or locate. The same article type can call for different compositions. For a hybrid paper choose one main structure and use a second form inside a module only where it clarifies the evidence. Give the author a specific recommendation.

| Manuscript and central question | Suggested form | What to draw and check |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Narrative review organized around a single topic | Radial map, segmented circle or grouped concept map | Put the disease, organ or concept at the center. Assign distinct facets to sectors. A circle can group parallel ideas. Add a returning arrow only when the literature establishes a cycle. |
| 2. Review comparing mechanisms, interventions or theories | Aligned columns or branches from a shared starting point | Apply the same comparison dimensions to each column, such as target, setting, evidence and limitation. Give unequal evidence strength an explicit label. |
| 3. Review about change over time | Timeline, disease-course strip or stage panels | Label whether the axis shows clinical time, treatment timing or the history of research. Cross-sectional differences between separate samples alone cannot establish transitions. |
| 4. Systematic review or meta-analysis | Question and evidence scope leading to synthesis, with a compact forest plot where justified | Include the comparator, endpoint, estimate and interval when reported. Preserve material heterogeneity, bias or low certainty. A review without pooling can show the direction and consistency of its evidence. Leave complete screening counts in the study flowchart unless selection drives the claim. |
| 5. Observational clinical study using hospital or public data | Population and question, measurement scenes, main comparison, result | Draw how the exposure and outcome were obtained. Link a test sample to the assay and a self-reported diagnosis to the questionnaire. Show how these inputs enter the analysis. State the design and express associations through comparisons and estimates. |
| 6. Dual-population or multi-cohort study | Parallel lanes or aligned panels | Keep each population's measurements, branches and results in its own lane. Share a scale only if the unit, comparator and estimand support comparison. Distinguish repeating an association from testing an already developed prediction model. |
| 7. Clinical prediction model | Inputs, development, completed validation and performance | Label where training, tuning and evaluation occurred. Place discrimination, calibration or other reported performance with the relevant data split. Feature attribution retains its model-specific meaning. Never imply external validation from an internal split. |
| 8. GEO or other public omics analysis | Discovery, replication where performed, biological interpretation | Separate discovery datasets from independent replication. Identify what differential analysis, enrichment, network analysis or selection actually found. Label enriched pathways as inferred. When no validation occurred, end at discovery or interpretation. |
| 9. Public omics combined with bench experiments | Discovery and candidate selection feeding a distinct experimental panel | Draw the cell, tissue or animal, perturbation, control and readout actually used. An expression assay can verify an expression difference. It cannot by itself verify the whole inferred pathway. |
| 10. Basic experimental research | Evidence-supported mechanism, or sequential or matched experimental comparisons | Connect each mechanistic arrow to its perturbation and readout. Show proposed steps as hypotheses with a legend. Use parallel experiments if the paper supports several observations without a single regulatory chain. |
| 11. Bibliometric study | Literature scope, theme or collaboration map, then trends or evidenced gaps | Select a simplified network, timeline or trend band from the reported analysis. Define corpus, dates, nodes, edges, weights and their units. Citation position or publication growth describes the literature. Limit clinical implications to what was actually studied. |

**Check the visual grammar.** Follow a connector while reading its verb aloud, such as “sampled,” “derived,” “compared,” “replicated” or “regulated.” Change the mark or add a short label if the picture suggests a stronger claim. Draw scenes that distinguish research actions even before the small labels are read. A generic patient icon beside an outcome rarely explains how either variable was measured.

**Design guidance.** A defined reading order and a self-contained main finding apply across these layouts. Journal-specific limits, including permitted illustration assistance, apply to the named journal.
