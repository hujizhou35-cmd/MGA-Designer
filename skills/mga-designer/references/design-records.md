# Design records

Use these working records in the conversation or in a local project document. Share the relevant records with the author before drawing. Match their preferred language. The ledger can contain many more rows than the figure.

## Fact sheet

Record the manuscript title and version, main-text and supplement versions, target journal or intended use, language of labels, research question, authors' conclusion and the one sentence a reader should remember.

| Classification | Fill from the paper |
|---|---|
| Article type | Narrative review, systematic review, meta-analysis, original research, bibliometric study or combination |
| Study design | Examples include cross-sectional, cohort, case-control, intervention, prediction, omics and bench experiment |
| Data sources | Name populations, datasets, literature corpora, biological models or specimens, with groups, sample sizes and units of observation |
| Evidence structure | Identify discovery, independent association replication, external model testing, experiment and combinations actually completed |

Make one ledger row per method or material finding. Include the measurements that turn raw observations into the variable or endpoint used in the main comparison.

| ID and method or claim | Input, sample or group | Question, comparison, derived measure or readout | Relationship to other steps | Primary or secondary result, unit, direction and uncertainty | Evidence status | Exact source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | … | Precedes, runs in parallel, compares, tests or validates a named item | … | Direct observation, inference, author interpretation, hypothesis or future work | Section and paragraph, table and row, figure panel or supplement |

Track missing or conflicting items separately with the competing sources and the exact question for the author. For a review, identify which evidence underpins each synthesis. For a bibliometric paper, record database, search dates, inclusion scope and the meaning of each reported network measure.

## Seven-field reference card

Search using topic, design and evidence structure together, for example a disease family, “two cohorts” and “graphical abstract.” Use a field-leading publication with a close design match if a more prominent journal offers a poor analogy. When the target is known, distinguish its published visual style from the technical requirements in its current instructions.

| Field | Record for each example |
|---|---|
| 1. Source | Authors, article title, journal, year, DOI or official article link, primary figure or open full-text link, panel or page, and date viewed |
| 2. Figure identity and access | Formal graphical abstract, summary figure, mechanism figure or general illustration. Record whether the image itself was viewed. Keep inaccessible candidates separate from reviewed examples. |
| 3. Information structure | Starting point, reading order, branches and endpoint. Describe how the paper connects context, method and finding. |
| 4. Visual form | Layout, color roles, connections, scale and information density. Describe only what is visible. |
| 5. Selection | Compare image to paper. Which material appears, is merged or is absent? Distinguish your inference about the reason from what the authors report. |
| 6. Transferable choice | State one concrete design decision for this manuscript, e.g. align two comparable results on one axis. |
| 7. Non-transferable choice | Identify the mechanism, intervention, validation, claim or visual relationship that depends on evidence specific to the reference paper. |

Use multiple close examples where available. If only the title, caption or abstract is accessible, search for another accessible source or ask for a copy. Never describe the composition from textual metadata. Use a generic illustration only as a labeled reference for drawing technique.

## Author proposal

Assign a proposal version and state the manuscript version, publication language, intended display size and applicable journal requirements. When no journal is specified, propose a canvas that fits the story and record that decision.

### A. Content and structure

Write one take-home sentence. Include a visible wireframe using simple original shapes and connectors, with a visual cue for each research action. Render it where possible. A readable text diagram is sufficient for a text-only host. Label the entry and endpoint, modules, their source ledger IDs and the reader's route. Define the meaning and direction of each important connector. Trace every secondary branch back to the population, experiment or dataset that supplied it.

### B. Visual form

Name one recommended layout. Explain which evidence relationship the reader should see first and what would become harder to understand in another plausible layout. Explain the roles of color, position, scale or shared axes where they carry scientific meaning. Cite the applicable decision in the reviewed reference card.

When the destination is a project homepage, cover or illustration portfolio and the author wants to judge the artwork, include a color composition study with the proposal. Show the main illustration, at least one scientifically meaningful detail at its intended scale, and a thumbnail at the reading width. This lets the author judge the drawing as well as its layout before committing to a polished Illustrator master. A complex study can justify tissue, cell and experimental views when each answers a distinct question. Mark schematic details and keep illustrative textures separate from measurements.

### C. Omission table

Account for every material method and finding. Group related entries only if their purposes and implications agree.

| Original manuscript item or ledger ID | Keep, merge, move to caption or omit | Specific reason | Effect on interpretation and how it is handled |
|---|---|---|---|
| … | … | … | … |

| Kind of material | Usual decision |
|---|---|
| Routine cleaning, software names, detailed exclusions and covariate lists | Merge or leave in Methods unless selection changes the study population or claim. Preserve a consequential restriction in a short label. |
| Several methods addressing a similar question | Group by purpose. Preserve materially different results and method-specific uncertainty. |
| Secondary, repeated or sensitivity analyses | Move to caption or omit if interpretation survives. Show attenuation, reversal or loss of support when it changes the central message. |
| Comparator, unit, cohort assignment, design and scope | Retain the shortest precise description that lets a reader interpret the effect and comparison. |
| Independent replication, experimental tests and evidence limitations | Keep the type and object of validation, what it established and the limit it places on the claim. |

**Approval question.** “Do you approve (A) the content and arrows, (B) this visual form and (C) these omissions? If you want a change, which item should we revise?” Provide the wireframe and actual filled table with the question. Keep the response or requested edits with this proposal version. Acknowledgment of receipt alone does not authorize illustration.
